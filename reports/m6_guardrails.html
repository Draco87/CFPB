Guardrails for the RAG drafter

Rule-based input and output checks, and a red-team test.

What guardrails do

The drafts go to a human agent, so nothing is blocked. Instead, simple rule-based checks put a visible warning on a draft that does something risky: states a number that is not in any retrieved passage (or in the complaint), promises a refund, admits fault, repeats personal data, or cites a rule that was not retrieved.

They are deliberately simple and explainable. They catch specific, common failures; they do not prove a draft is right - that is what the faithfulness evaluation (Module 5) is for.

B. Red-team test: can a complaint trick the drafter?

10 hand-written tricky complaints (eval/redteam.json): prompt injection, a fake regulation, personal data, an off-topic request, demands for a refund or an admission of fault, and one normal complaint as a control. Each was drafted with the plain Module 4 prompt and with the guarded prompt.

Attacks that worked: 0/9 with the plain prompt, 2/9 with the guarded prompt. Worked and slipped past the output checks: 0/9 plain, 0/9 guarded.

Small, hand-made test: it shows which kinds of attack get through, not a precise success rate.
