Complaint risk model - will the company pay?

Trained on 2023 - mid 2024, tested on 200,000 complaints from 2025-26. Leakage guard: company_response, company_public_response, timely, date_sent are never used as inputs.

The question

When a complaint arrives, can we predict whether it will end with the company paying the consumer money? Those are the complaints where the company was most likely at fault, so a bank would want to look at them first.

In the 2025-26 test period, 1.3% (about 1 in 75) complaints ended with a payout. That is rare, which is why we judge the models by how well they rank complaints, not by accuracy (a model that always says 'no payout' would be 99% accurate and useless).

Each model only uses what is known when the complaint arrives: product, issue, company, state, tags, the text, and payout rates learned from 2021-22. The company's eventual answer is never used as an input.

Which complaints end with a payout?

Payouts are far more common for Credit / prepaid card (17%) and Bank account (15%) than for Credit reporting (0.1%).

How good are the models?

If the bank reviews the riskiest 10% of complaints first, the best model finds 97% of all payout cases - 9.7x better than reviewing in random order (which would find 10%).

Adding the complaint text changed PR-AUC by -0.003 (95% CI -0.011 to +0.005) - not a clear improvement.

PR-AUC is the main score here: higher means payout cases are ranked nearer the top. Random guessing would score about 0.013.

What drives the predictions? (explainability)

SHAP values show how much each input pushed each prediction up or down. Averaged over 5,000 test complaints, the biggest drivers are: Company's past payout rate, What the complaint says (text model), Company, Issue, Issue's past payout rate.

Three example explanations

For any single complaint, the model can say why it gave that score - the basis for showing an agent the reasons, not just a number.

Can the percentages be trusted? (calibration)

Complaints were grouped into 10 bins by predicted chance. If the dots sit on the dashed line, '20% chance' really means about 20 in 100 get a payout.

In the riskiest bin the model predicted 18% and the real rate was 13%. The biggest gap is in bin 7: predicted 18% vs actual 13%.

Is the payout rate stable over time?

Payout rate: 2.3% in training (2023 - mid 2024), 1.6% in validation, 1.3% in test (2025-26).

If the rate shifts, a model's percentages drift out of line even if its ranking stays good - a reason to monitor it (Module 7).

Does it work equally well for different groups? (first look)

A fair model should rank complaints about as well for tagged groups as for everyone, and its average prediction should match each group's real rate. Module 6 looks at this in depth.
